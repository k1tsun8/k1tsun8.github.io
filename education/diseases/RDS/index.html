Removal Denial Syndrome

Removal Denial Syndrome (RDS) is a secondary effect of Red Floods experienced by relatives and friends of Removed victims. It generally takes hold once most (or all) memories and records of the Removed victim have completely faded from reality. It is characterized by a few distressing feelings commonly reported by survivors of Red Floods:

  • Longing for someone you don't think you'll ever meet
  • Lingering feeling like you're forgetting something
  • Constantly feeling like someone's name is on the tip of your tongue
  • Confusion about inexplicable blank papers or documents scattered around the home
  • Confusion about living in a home meant for more people than currently living in it

If you experience any of these sensations (or similar ones), please avoid future Red Flood events at all costs. Removal Denial Syndrome is a sign that you may be vulnerable to hallucinations during Red Floods. Victims of RDS often report the following secondary effects which only seem to occur during active Red Floods:

  • Hearing familiar voices you can't recognize
  • Seeing familiar body parts in Red Floodwaters
  • Seeing familiar clothing that doesn't belong to you
  • Seeing familiar faces or bodies in Red Floodwaters

It is presumed that the perceived familiar features are those of the relevant Removed victims, but there is currently no known way to confirm this theory. Victims of RDS tend to vehemently argue that the people they see in Red Floodwaters are in fact people they once knew, but they usually are unable to identify them by name.

Victims of RDS who experience these hallucinations often report higher levels of distress and frustration at their inability to recall their loved ones' identity. It has been known to cause other severe mental health conditions (including extreme depression, anxiety, and paranoia) if not treated properly by a professional. Please learn how to protect yourself against Red Floods if you're suffering (or have ever suffered from) RDS.
